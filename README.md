# Capital Conversion, Debt and Interconnectivity: A Formal Approach to Inequality among Nomadic Herders in Ancient Eurasia
The repository contains the supplementary materials for the paper "*Capital Conversion, Debt and Interconnectivity: A formal Approach to Inequality among Nomadic Herders in Ancient Eurasia*".

### Abstract
The cemeteries of the Xiongnu empire reveal marked and persistent inequality. Yet herd wealth is volatile, and scholars disagree on whether durable hierarchy could arise within steppe society or required external resources and coercion. Building on Miller’s (2024) account of the Xiongnu political economy, this paper asks whether debt, that is, the conversion of livestock into social obligations through herd-sharing contracts, could by itself generate lasting inequality, and how access to external resources through interconnectivity networks changes the outcome. These questions are addressed through an Agent-Based Model (ABM) of herding households built in NetLogo. In the model, exchange keeps the community alive and debt makes its top more stable, but a hierarchy that lasts for generations emerges only when the lent animals remain the patron’s, even though clients leave as soon as their own herds recover. Debt keeps no more households alive than the gift: the community benefits from the transfer of animals, while hierarchy comes from the obligations attached to it. External resources neither create nor replace this hierarchy, and make it more durable only when they pass through those already at the top.

**Keywords:** Agent-Based Modeling, ABM, Xiongnu, Inequality, Nomadic Pastoralists, Eurasia.
